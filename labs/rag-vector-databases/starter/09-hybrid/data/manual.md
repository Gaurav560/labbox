# Vanrai Hills Wildlife Sanctuary: Field Manual

The field manual for rangers, guards and volunteers of the Vanrai Hills Wildlife Sanctuary, 212 square kilometres of
evergreen forest, grassland and river valley in the Western Ghats. Every section has a number; quote it in reports.

## FM-1.1 The three zones
The sanctuary is divided into three zones. The core zone (148 square kilometres) is closed to visitors and may be entered
only by staff on duty and by researchers with a permit. The buffer zone (52 square kilometres) surrounds the core and
contains the six villages that use the forest for grazing and firewood under the joint forest committee rules. The
tourism zone (12 square kilometres) is the only area open to safari vehicles. Zone boundaries are marked with painted
stones: red for the core, yellow for the buffer and white for the tourism zone.

## FM-1.2 Gates and checkposts
There are three gates. Ghatmatha Gate on the eastern ridge is the main entrance for visitors and the only gate where safari
permits are sold. River Gate in the south is for staff, forest produce and emergency vehicles. Kolhi Gate in the north is
closed to all vehicles from 15 June to 30 September. Every gate keeps an entry register: name, vehicle number, purpose,
time in and time out. A vehicle that has not left by closing time is reported to the control room at once.

## FM-1.3 The control room
The control room at the Range Forest Office in Vanrai village is staffed day and night. Its call sign is Vanrai Base and
it listens on radio channel 4. Channel 6 is kept free for emergencies. Every patrol checks in with Vanrai Base at the
start, every 30 minutes on night patrol, and at the end. The control room keeps the master map, the patrol roster and the
camera trap register.

## FM-2.1 Beats and patrol routes
The sanctuary is divided into twelve beats, B-01 to B-12. Beats B-01 to B-08 lie in the core zone, B-09 to B-11 in the
buffer zone and B-12 is the tourism zone. Each beat has a guard and a fixed patrol route of 8 to 14 kilometres, walked
on foot at least four days a week. Routes change every three months so that poachers cannot learn the timings.

## FM-2.2 Night patrols
Night patrols go out in pairs, never alone, and carry a torch, a radio, a first aid kit and the beat map. The pair
checks in with Vanrai Base every 30 minutes. If a check-in is missed twice, the control room sends the nearest vehicle
to the last known position. Night patrols do not leave the patrol route and do not follow animals off the path.

## FM-2.3 The patrol log
Every patrol fills in the patrol log on the M-Patrol app or, without a phone signal, on paper Form PL-1. Record the beat,
the start and end time, the distance walked, every animal or sign seen with its grid reference, and anything unusual:
cut trees, snares, fresh fire, strangers or vehicles. Logs are uploaded or handed in within 24 hours.

## FM-3.1 Camera traps
The sanctuary runs 60 camera traps, numbered CT-101 to CT-160, mostly on animal paths and near water holes. Each trap is
fixed to a tree at knee height, 3 to 4 metres from the path, facing north or south so that the sun does not trigger it.
Traps are numbered on a metal tag and listed in the camera trap register with their beat and grid reference.

## FM-3.2 Batteries and memory cards
Change the batteries of every camera trap every 21 days, and swap the memory card at the same visit. Write the date and
the trap number on the card envelope. Cards go to the control room the same day, where images are copied, backed up and
tagged by species. Never delete images in the field, even blank ones.

## FM-3.3 A missing or damaged camera trap
If a camera trap is missing or broken, photograph the tree and the ground around it, do not touch any marks, and report
it to Vanrai Base by radio at once. Then fill in Form CT-9 within 24 hours. A missing trap is treated as a possible sign
of poaching: the beat is patrolled daily for the next two weeks.

## FM-4.1 General rules for animal encounters
Most animals avoid people. Encounters happen when an animal is surprised at close range, is with young, or is
cornered. Keep a distance, talk quietly so that animals know you are there, and never stand between a mother and her
young. Report every close encounter in the patrol log with the time, the place and what the animal did.

## FM-4.2 Meeting a tiger
If you meet a tiger, stand still, keep facing it and do not run: running can trigger a chase. Do not crouch or turn your
back. Back away slowly while the tiger watches you, and leave it a clear path to move away. Tigers in Vanrai are shy and
usually leave within a minute. Report the sighting to Vanrai Base with the beat and grid reference.

## FM-4.3 Leopards near villages
Leopards live close to the buffer zone villages and hunt dogs, goats and calves at night. They are seen far more often
than tigers. If a leopard is seen inside a village, keep people back, do not try to drive it away with crowds or stones,
and call Vanrai Base. The rapid response team decides whether to wait, guide it out or, rarely, set a cage.

## FM-4.4 Gaur
Gaur, the Indian bison, graze in the grasslands near Kolhi Gate at dawn and dusk. A gaur weighs up to a tonne and will
charge if it feels trapped, especially a lone bull. Keep at least 50 metres away, never walk between a gaur and its herd,
and move behind a large tree if a gaur lowers its head and paws the ground.

## FM-4.5 Sloth bears
The sloth bear injures more people in India than any other wild animal, because it attacks when surprised at close
range. In bear areas walk in pairs, talk or clap as you walk, and take care near termite mounds and fruiting trees. If
a bear stands up or charges, do not run: make yourself look large, shout, and keep your face protected.

## FM-4.6 Snakebite
Russell's viper, the spectacled cobra, the common krait and the saw-scaled viper all live in the sanctuary. After a
bite, keep the person still and calm, take off rings and watches, and keep the bitten limb still and at rest. Do not cut
the wound, suck it, apply ice or tie a tourniquet. Carry the person to the Primary Health Centre at Rajur, which keeps
anti-venom, and call Vanrai Base on the way so that the centre is told before you arrive.

## FM-4.7 Elephants
A small herd of eight to twelve wild elephants moves through the southern beats, B-05 to B-07, between October and
January. Give elephants at least 100 metres. Never block their path, never use fire crackers near them without the Range
Forest Officer's order, and turn back if you hear trumpeting or branches breaking close ahead.

## FM-5.1 The fire season
The fire season runs from 15 February to 31 May, when the grass is dry and the deciduous trees drop their leaves. Fire
lines, strips 6 metres wide cleared of grass and leaves, are cut along every road and beat boundary before 15 February.
During the fire season no cooking fires are allowed anywhere in the sanctuary, including at the gates.

## FM-5.2 Fire watch towers
Four fire watch towers, T1 to T4, are staffed from 10:00 to 18:00 every day of the fire season. A watcher who sees smoke
radios Vanrai Base with the tower number, the compass bearing and a rough distance. When two towers report the same
smoke, the crossing of their bearings gives the fire's position on the master map.

## FM-5.3 Fighting a ground fire
Ground fires are fought by a crew of at least four, with fire beaters, rakes and leaf blowers, from the burnt side and
never from the unburnt side ahead of the fire. Keep a clear way out behind the crew. Never fight a crown fire, one that
has reached the tops of the trees: move everyone to a fire line or a burnt area and wait for the fire to reach it.

## FM-6.1 Safari timings and vehicles
Safaris run in the tourism zone from 06:30 to 10:00 and from 15:00 to 18:00, every day except Tuesdays, from 1 October
to 14 June. Only registered open vehicles with a licensed sanctuary guide may enter, at most 24 vehicles in each session.
The speed limit inside the sanctuary is 20 kilometres an hour; vehicles may not leave the marked roads.

## FM-6.2 Visitor rules
Visitors stay inside their vehicle except at the two marked viewpoints. Plastic bags and bottles, loud music, feeding
animals and picking plants are not allowed. Visitors who break the rules are taken back to Ghatmatha Gate and may be
fined under the sanctuary's rules. Guides report rule breaking in their trip sheet.

## FM-6.3 Drones and photography permits
Flying a drone anywhere over the sanctuary is forbidden without a written permit. Apply on Form P-7 to the Deputy
Conservator of Forests at least 30 days before the flight, with the purpose, the area, the dates and the pilot's
licence. Commercial film and photography shoots also need a P-7 permit and pay a daily fee. Rangers who see an unpermitted
drone note its direction and time and inform Vanrai Base.

## FM-6.4 Research permits
Researchers apply to the Deputy Conservator with their study plan, the beats they need and the dates. A research permit
allows entry into the core zone on foot, always with a sanctuary guard. Researchers share a copy of their data and any
camera trap images with the control room, and they report sightings of rare species within 48 hours.

## FM-6.5 The monsoon closure
From 15 June to 30 September heavy rain closes the tourism zone and makes most roads unsafe. Safaris stop, Kolhi Gate is
closed, and only River Gate stays open for staff and emergencies. Foot patrols continue in every beat, but river
crossings are not attempted when the water is above knee height.

## FM-7.1 Snares and traps
Poachers set wire snares on animal paths, often near crop fields at the forest edge. Search for them on every patrol. When
you find a snare, photograph it where it is, record the grid reference, then remove it and keep it as evidence in a
labelled bag. A snare with a trapped animal is reported to Vanrai Base at once; do not try to free a large animal alone.

## FM-7.2 Signs of poaching
Treat these as signs of poaching and report them the same day: a missing or damaged camera trap, fresh cut marks on
trees, shots heard at night, strangers or vehicles on forest roads after closing time, animal parts for sale in a
village, and vultures gathering over one place. Write down vehicle numbers, faces and times, but never confront an armed
group: withdraw and call for help.

## FM-7.3 Finding a dead animal
If you find a dead tiger, leopard, elephant or gaur, do not touch it or let anyone near it. Photograph it from a
distance, guard the site, and inform Vanrai Base at once. A veterinary officer and the Range Forest Officer must attend,
and the body is examined before it is disposed of. For smaller animals, record the species, the place and the likely
cause in the patrol log.

## FM-8.1 Crop and livestock losses
Wild pigs, gaur and deer damage crops in the buffer zone villages, and leopards kill goats, dogs and calves. A farmer
who loses crops or livestock to a wild animal can claim compensation. The claim must reach the Range Forest Office within
48 hours, with photographs, the village name and a panchnama, a written record of the loss signed by two witnesses.

## FM-8.2 Helping a claim
The beat guard visits the place within 24 hours of hearing about a loss, confirms the animal from tracks and marks, and
writes the inspection note on Form HW-3. Be fair and quick: a farmer who waits weeks for compensation is less likely to
support the sanctuary. Never promise an amount; the Range Forest Officer decides.

## FM-9.1 First aid kit
Every patrol carries a first aid kit with bandages, sterile dressings, an elastic bandage for snakebite immobilisation,
antiseptic, pain relief tablets, oral rehydration salts, a foil blanket and a whistle. Check the kit at the start of
every month and refill it at the control room. Report every injury, even a small one, in the patrol log.

## FM-9.2 Radios and phones
Radios are charged every night at the beat camp. Mobile phones work only on the eastern ridge and near the villages; do
not rely on them inside the core zone. If your radio fails, return to the nearest high point or road and try again; if
you cannot reach Vanrai Base, return to the beat camp before dark.
